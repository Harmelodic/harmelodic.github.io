# Principles vs Practicality

Should we do things "properly" and abide by our values, principles and morality? Or should we do things practically in
order to get the job done?

This is seemingly an endless debate amongst software engineers - and like everything, it's all about trade-offs and
taking a balanced approach:

1. Know and set your values, principles.
2. Identify the problem, the ideal solution and the ideal method of achieving it.
3. Identify what aspects of that solution & method are most "painful" (i.e. they are difficult, time-consuming, etc.).
4. Identify what "painful" aspects you cannot avoid or believe you shouldn't budge on because the principle is worth the
   pain (this is the most difficult bit, and involves a bunch of discussion and difficult decisions - remember to avoid
   assumptions or confirm them with data / experienced perspective).
5. Prioritise tasks based on greatest need and impact.
6. Build iteratively to allow changing course earlier, and to maximise the chance the solution is fit-for-purpose.

Too often I've seen projects suffer because people have a grand principled vision that is not grounded in reality, and
either (a) won't be achievable or (b) won't bring the value it needs to bring quick enough to justify the time, cost and
people.

Too often I've seen projects suffer because people have a too practical mindset to engineering that results in quick
wins, but a poorly-built solution that is costly to maintain and improve and/or solves a subset of the problems that it
should be solving - and sometimes the engineers responsible for those solutions use practicality as an excuse for
lazy/poor engineering.

Be principled from the start. Choose practicality when it makes sense. Then get it done and own the thing you build.
