# Being Openly Vulnerable

> "I'm having hard time."
>
> "I don't know."
>
> "That frustrates me."
>
> "I disagree."

These are all examples of statements that put on in an openly, socially-vulnerable situation.

Being openly vulnerable has it's pros and cons, but I personally believe that it is better to be openly vulnerable (most
of the time) than it is to attempt to hide it.

## Pros

We're all human. To hide our vulnerability is to pretend / put on facade for others to show that we don't feel
vulnerable, which sets a precedent and expectation that other's should do the same. It's already uncomfortable enough to
show vulnerability, but with a precedent to hide vulnerability in place it becomes even more uncomfortable to open up to
being vulnerable.

If we instead open ourselves up to being vulnerable, then we are usually faced with empathy from other people (not all
the time, more on that later). This not helps tackle our feels of vulnerability with compassion and solidarity, but also
showcases to everyone else that it's OK to be vulnerable in the social space we find ourselves in. The more it occurs,
the safer and more comfortable we feel in the spaces we find ourselves in.

In summary, we hide our vulnerability to avoid discomfort, but in reality, we end up being more comfortable when living
a life where express our vulnerability.

## Cons

Some people are dickheads. These people take advantage of vulnerable people and safe spaces to bully, strongman,
intimidate or simply manipulate their way into a position of power.

So, the con to being openly vulnerable, is that it opens up opportunities for dickheads to be dickheads. Thankfully, the
counter to this is not "don't be vulnerable" but instead: Have courage and actively resist and discourage the behaviour
of the dickhead.

It is not often done so openly maliciously, and the dickhead is often not even aware they are being a dickhead. Take
care in these situations to not call out accidentally dickhead-ery (it's OK to make mistakes after all) but it is still
important to showcase that dickhead-y behaviour is not socially accepted.

## Being vulnerable

Here's some guidance on how to actively, showcase open vulnerability to help set the precedent for others:

- When you didn't know something, remark that you didn't know it, and it was pleasant to learn.
	- This indicates to other people that it's OK to not know things, and that learning is a natural part of life and
	  work.

- When you don't know how to do something, remark that you're unfamiliar with it and that you might need to ask for
  help, or take a little more time in order to learn how to do it.
	- This expresses truth and reduces the pressure on yourself to show competence in a situation you're not competent
	  at.
	- This also makes it easier to ask for help later, since people already know that you might.

- When you're giving an opinion, with other people (especially when in a discussion or discourse), use language that
  _shows_ it is an opinion. For example, try not to omit the phrase "I think..." when giving opinions, or add things
  like "from my perspective".
	- This ensures that opinions don't come across as facts, when they aren't. This then makes it easier for people to
	  express their own opinions and a discussion to occur, as opposed to others feeling intimidated that your opinion
	  is fact and so become less likely to voice their concern or disagreement.

- Because things can be misremembered, when sharing knowledge from memory, add clarifications and caveats like "I think
  that's right", or "I might be misremembering that, but I'm quite sure about it".
	- This provides an opportunity for others to voice their disagreement in case they remember differently, which then
	  provides an opportunity to verify the knowledge if there is disagreement.

- When you find yourself in disagreement, voice your disagreement politely.
	- This showcases to others that disagreement is OK, and showcases how disagreement should be conducted.

## Preventing dickheads

Watch out for the following behaviour:

- Being directive / autocratic / instructive.
- Being overconfident / certain in times of uncertainty.
- Initiating conflict and being derogatory.
- Showing a lack of kindness or understanding.
- Being more focused on "taking action" than on creating a safe space for discussion and consensus.
- Overriding or dismissing other's opinions.
- Not providing opportunities for others to help / take on responsibility / offer their advice (thereby centralising and
  safeguarding their position of power).
- Not being transparent (especially when prompted) with their intentions.
- Disregarding others' values and/or seeking to supplant them with their own values through coercive means (e.g. money,
  bullying, overriding).
- Being vindictive / spiteful.
- Intolerant views and behaviour.

Once identified, then attempt to mitigate the issue by standing up to it:

- Discussing with the dickhead how their behaviour is affecting others.
- If no empathy or understanding is shown, then make it clear that their behaviour is not accepted.
- If no change occurs in their behaviour, then action should be taken (depending on the context) to mitigate the
  behaviour of the dickhead.
