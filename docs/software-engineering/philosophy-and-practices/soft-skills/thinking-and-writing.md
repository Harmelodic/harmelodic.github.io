# Thinking and Writing

When reflecting on critical thinking and the writing process, I once read a statement along the lines of:

> To think is to write, and to write is to think.

## To think is to write

This means that in order to fully think through points of view then one must write them down.

This requires us to take thoughts which may be unstructured, contain biases or even contradictions and put them through
the writing process, thereby subjecting them to more critical review to bring about structure and to aid identifying and
removing any biases and contradictions.

## To write is to think

This means that when one is confused, wants to reflect or come up with ideas then one can attempt to write down what
thoughts they already have to see what further opinions and points of view are realised as part of the writing process.

## It does not have to be writing

Thanks to my good friend Anton, who also likes writing, for sparking the question:

> What other kind of activities are there that make (other) people think?

First off, by "thinking" I mean: Unpacking / reflecting upon stuff. It's effectively taking a thing ("an idea" or "a
concept") and trying to:

- analyse what aspects it has (e.g. deconstructing it, noting properties of it, matching aspects of it to other things,
  what biases may affect it, how it might interact with other things, or whether it's ethical/moral)
- develop it using other ideas, concepts, experience, assumptions into something more useful or interesting than the
  original thing - or if it was a problem, how we might solve it.
- imagine how it might manifest into a real tangible thing, and discovering new ideas or problems that would appear upon
  doing so, and subsequently repeat the process (kind of like development, but it's more... "post-development"
  development)

There are other forms of thinking (learning, judging, computation) but I'm ignoring that, since I'm talking about the
sort of thinking that one does when one is writing.

So, analyse, develop, imagine...

How can we otherwise manifest analysis? Experimentation through science (e.g. physical experiments, social experiments),
discussion with others, discussion with yourself, visualisation through drawing or painting (hm, probably the reason I
love drawing on whiteboards).

How to otherwise manifest development? Taking manifested analysis and then changing it (in writing, we're essentially
just refactoring or rewriting). In experiments, this would be changing a variable and rerunning the experiment, or
in discussion then we bring in someone new to the discussion or consider a new perspective, or redraw or modify the
visualisation from its current state.

How to otherwise manifest imagination? Other than "more of the same" kinds of development... I think activities that
introduce some kind of boredom or idle-mindedness is important. Writing is inherently a slow and laborious task to form
words and phrases to represent the thoughts you have, and so you have time to imagine other things. When we day-dream,
or do jigsaws, or read, or go for a walk, or paint, or noodle on a guitar, or sleep, where we let our mind go idle for a
bit. Then that seems to spark imagination and inventiveness, and we do that "post-development" development on the things
that are sat in the back of our minds.

So... it doesn't have to be writing, but any activity where you can (a) actually manifest the idea, then (b) be able to
change it, then (c) also give yourself time to be idle with the idea too (during or after). Writing, for me, seems like
an easy way to achieve those things.

The wonderful extra benefit you get with writing is that you can then share it with others, and they can understand
(almost) exactly what you're thinking by simply reading what you've written.
