# Rising Above Ego

Rough thoughts / general vibe:

- People have egos / vanity / pride / their own ambitions.
- These can be useful motivators, and can drive people to improve their skills and/or create a bigger impact.
- Usually ego is satisfied by roles/titles, or by higher salary, or by bonuses, or by being given more responsibility /
  power.
- However, the goal of making software (or doing anything at work, really) is to _make things better_ (or however you
  want to phrase it).
	- Could say "be helpful", but sometimes you're being helpful and not being properly rewarded for that and so in
	  order to make things better, would be to stop being exploited for being helpful, but instead fix the system.
	- Could say "get the work done", but that has the same problem as "be helpful" - or maybe the work itself is not
	  making things better, but worse (e.g. enshittification or environmental destruction).
	- Maybe "do good", but that can get pretty subjective.
	- Even "make things better" is subjective and has nuance - the idea of making software might not even be "making
	  things better" if people were happy and satisfied with the non-software-driven method.
	- Maybe "improve quality of life"? That's getting real abstract and moral now... but maybe that's the point? Bah,
	  language is hard.
- Sacrificing time, effort, or actual results of "making things better", in order to satisfy one's ego (e.g. achieve a
  certain salary, or be promoted, or be given a specific role/title, or "win" an argument) is to selfishly put oneself
  first before the goal, which I think is morally reprehensible.
- An organisation that allows for this to happen (via improper incentives, or structure, etc.) should be changed.
	- This doesn't mean all titles, hierarchy or salary differences are immoral. Formal structure in an organisation is
	  still an important mechanism to the social-cohesion of an organisation and helps facilitate work being carried out
	  and responsibility & accountability is upheld.
	- But when a software engineer is in fair circumstances (pay, role, title, etc.) and is placed in a situation where
	  their ego/self/position could be better satisfied at the cost of "making things better", then the engineer should
	  pick "make things better" and strive to encourage others to do the same.
- Also, you can't remove your ego, or ignore it. We often think egotistically, or get wound up in our own heads and our
  ego wants to be satisfied, even though we're in fair circumstances. In these situations, we can try to think about our
  circumstances and the people around you and be mindful and thankful/grateful for what we have.
	- Also, sometimes we think everything is fine and be ignoring systemic injustices that are affecting others, or just
	  that we think we're in fair circumstances, but we're actually not, and we should instead be trying
	  to [change the status quo](./changing-the-status-quo.md) (and thus "making things better").
	- So, are you right to not feel satisfied? Or should you be happy with what you've got? Reflection, therapy and
	  talking about these things honestly with friends and colleagues should help you decipher which situation you're
	  in.
