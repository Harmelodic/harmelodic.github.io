# Option Making

When making decisions, you need to consider your options. In order to consider options, you need to have options.

"Option making" is that.

Fundamentally, option making is an exercise in recognising the functional and non-functional requirements for a solution, and then coming up with solutions to fit those requirements. A workshop or some kind of ideation process is useful for this.

## Generic strategic options

When considering "strategy" problems that fit into "How do we move forward with something?", you usually have the following options:

- Do it now
- Do it later
- Somebody else does it now
- Somebody else does it later
- We/Somebody does something completely different, to avoid doing _this_ thing.
- We don't do it (and just continue with our current situation).

Plus some other ones, depending on your context.

## Architectural options

These are highly dependent on your situation, but the options should mostly (if not entirely) fit all requirements, and most likely will incorporate some kind of known architectural pattern.

Sometimes the problem is a novel one and the conventional architectural patterns don't exist, so you must invent it in order to have it as an option.
