# Onboarding

Onboarding should achieve two things:

- Provide the tooling and access needed for the job.
- Establish [alignment](../alignment.md).

Providing the tooling and access are operational tasks usually carried out by managers, IT, HR and other administrative
functions of an organisation.

Establishing alignment is to essentially educate the new person in the following areas to get the up to speed with how
the organisation functions (the operating model) and the philosophy (values, principles, and practices) and high-level
architecture currently present.

The person is usually joining a specific team that will have its own system architecture and team practices. This will
have its own onboarding alignment.

Documentation should be readily available, discoverable, mostly centralised and should be the same documentation as
everyone else uses - thus forcing documentation to be fit for everyone, is repeatedly referenceable, and doesn't become
fragmented.

Onboarding is an excellent opportunity to garner a new perspective on the existing organisation, philosophy,
architecture and implemented systems. Whilst achieving good alignment is necessary, it may be that the result is not to
align the person to the organisation's philosophy, but have the organisation accommodate or shift from the learnings it
gains from the new person's perspective. This can be achieved through regular check-ins or interviews with new people,
during and after onboarding and then feedback acted upon by the appropriate people (management, new people, etc.).
