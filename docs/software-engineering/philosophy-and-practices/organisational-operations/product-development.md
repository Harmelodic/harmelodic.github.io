# Product Development

a.k.a. product management, or project management, or ... how we go about making software from a "product" point of
view (not technical leadership or people management)

Use of the word product here is just to be generic, it could be:

- Actual manufactured product, that comes with software.
- Software product.
- Software service.
- Other service / offering that is backed by software.

Important things:

- User / Customer feedback
	- Want low friction, and few people between customer feedback and development team (big chains of bureaucracy leads
	  to feedback loss and poor decision-making / prioritisation)
- Ideation processes to generate novel ideas (e.g. hackathons, workshops, suggestion box, etc.)
- Domain-Driven Design (software engineers to work closely with business/domain experts (stream-aligned))
- Prioritisation of tasks (graph: Impact vs Effort)
	- Low-effort, low-impact? Ignore, unless it's the kind of thing that accumulates into high-impact, high-effort (you
	  want to solve it whilst it's low effort)
	- Low-effort, high-impact? Just do it.
	- High-effort, high-impact? Plan for it and progress iteratively.
	- High-effort, low-impact? Ignore until you have time (monitor impact status to ensure it doesn't get ignored).
- Iterative development, but don't forget the long-term strategy and non-functional requirements
- Not all work can be new product development work. Make time for:
	- Maintenance
	- Research & Learning & Knowledge-sharing
	- Revising existing ways of working
	- Reducing Toil
	- Addressing incidents
	- Reducing existing technical debt (though it's often better to not produce technical debt in the first place)
- Remember: as the product feature set grows, the more there is to maintain. This results in:
	- Costs going up (more to maintain / run) - can be counteracted by attempting cost-cutting measures (though this has
	  its own consequences)
	- Speed of development going down (time needs to be spent maintaining existing product) - can be counter-acted by
	  hiring more or providing better tooling (though this has its own consequences).
