# Administrative Functions

Workers do work to achieve the main business of the organisation (healthcare, manufacturing, a service, banking,
whatever).

This organisation of workers needs to be organised, and risk mitigated in order to ensure the continuity of the
organisation. To do this, organisations need to perform a variety of "functions" to oversee and enable (a.k.a.
administrate) the operation of the organisation.

- We tend to think of management / governance / administration in different "roles"
- Those roles have multiple functions
- If we break up the roles into just the pure functions, we can reorganise things differently, and invent different
  roles.
- Some roles contain functions that we actually don't want / value
- As we break up roles and deduce functions, we can classify functions on a Value spectrum (Needed, Wanted, Undesirable,
  etc.)
- As organisations ways of working and cultures shift, different functions get moved around (e.g. architecture is
  getting decentralised more nowadays) - which leaves question as to what roles actually do, and whether they are
  beneficial.
- Knowing functions of roles, and being able to reorganise when things change, keeps us well-functioning and may help
  us avoid bullshit jobs.

Example administrative roles of an organisation that makes software as part of its work, with their breakdown of
functions:

- HR
	- Hiring & Exiting
	- Facilities management (e.g. office management, supplies, safety, renovations, etc.)
	- IT services
	- Employee management tooling (e.g. Intranet, Timesheets)
	- Workplace policy (e.g. Diversity & Inclusion, General Code of Conduct, Work trips)
	- Employee benefits management
- Legal & Compliance
	- Legal advice
	- Case management
	- Legal / Compliance
	- Auditing / Self-Verification
- Finances
	- Accounting
	- Insurances
- Communications & Customer Relations
	- Public Relations
	- Marketing
	- Sales
	- Spinning (i.e. like a spin doctor)
	- Customer Service - Phone lines, email responses, live chat, general feedback.
	- Customer Due-diligence
- People Management
	- Career development
	- Conflict resolution
	- Continuous iteration of workers' "Ways of Working"
	- Co-ordination / compliance between the team/individuals and the other administrative functions of the company.
- Technical Leadership
	- Advice & Guidance of technical work in various fields:
		- Engineering Practices (mainly technical but some cross-over with "Ways of Working")
		- System Architecture
		- Data & Insights
		- Security
		- User Experience & UI Design
	- Identifying cross-functional technical issues and driving (facilitating and co-ordinating) initiatives to solve
	  them.
	- Formulating processes and guardrails to ensure high-quality, fast decisions.
- Business (or Product or Project) Management
	- Prioritisation of work
	- Facilitation and co-ordination of business-focused work
	- Business-work reporting
	- Business projections

I view these administrative functions from a "servant" management/leadership perspective where they have an emphasis on
facilitating and ensuring work is done, rather than making decisions on work or being directive/autocratic or engaging
in top-down management practices. Workers should be in charge of doing the work and making decisions, but administrative
functions organise workers and put processes into place to ensure those decisions and that work is done fairly, legally,
is of good-quality, is within budget, is on-time and all workers are happy.
