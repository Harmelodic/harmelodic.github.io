# Operating Models and Communication

Operating Models are the defined way that the organisation "operates". It's the definition of:

- How people are organised
- Who is responsible for what
- How work is expected to be conducted
- How we communicate and collaborate on that work

All operating models that aren't just "everyone does everything" results the divvying up of responsibilities. This helps
people focus on specific areas, but also will inevitably introduce siloing and poor communication / collaboration on
other areas.

Choosing an operating model, means choosing how to collaborate and where to silo, and how that siloing is mitigated when
boundaries need crossing to achieve a larger goal.

From one perspective, an operating model is just how we're organised, but from another perspective an operating model is
just a definition of how we naturally should be communicating with one another. Getting communication right can make the
difference between dysfunction and a well-operating organisation.

Things to cover / think about:

- Different Operating Model and how they affect communication and how work is achieved.
- The value of transparency, clear communication, and discoverable historic communication, and how to do them well.
- Communication rules to ensure reduced time wasted on poor communication and increased time spent on good communication
  and productivity (don't want to have people spending/wasting their days away just messaging people on
  Slack/Teams/etc. or reading and responding to email - but also want to use these tools effectively to collaborate).
- When to have meetings and when to not
