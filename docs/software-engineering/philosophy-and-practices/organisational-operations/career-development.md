# Career Development

## There is always more to learn

Try to learn as much as you can. Not only is learning fun but the gained knowledge and experience is makes you more
useful and thus employable.

Often referred to as having a "growth mindset", though I'm not a fan of that because:

1. Being content with yourself and not "growing" is a perfectly acceptable and healthy way of life.
2. The evangelism of the term associates "growth" with "good". When the growth term is applied to the economy, it is not
   always good. If you disagree, I encourage you to go read _Less is More_ by Jason Hickel.

## Conferences

Going to conferences can be useful. You can learn from others and bring knowledge back to the organisation, as well as
network (socially) with other people which can aid business relationships or open up job opportunities.

They are otherwise quite expensive to attend, often filled with not-so-helpful presentations and stalls that are little
more than advertising, and are environmentally unfriendly (travel emissions, mass-produced shit "swag",
energy-consuming).

I recommend going to smaller and ideally local conferences where the focus is on learning and networking.

Certification usually means taking a test and (if you pass) getting a certificate saying you are qualified to do a
thing.

Personally, I've not really seen much value in doing it and many certificates do not accurately reflect the actual skill
or ability of an engineer. However, they can be useful to make you appear more employable and in some cases it can be
mandatory due to regulations or company policy.

- [AWS Certification](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/)
