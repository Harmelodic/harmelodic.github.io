# Sunglasses

Sunglasses should fit your face, protect your eyes, be durable, made
from [sustainable materials](sustainable-materials.md), and provide the aesthetic you're looking for.

## Fit / Sizing

You want your sunglasses to be wide enough on your face so they sit comfortably and not be too tight or too big, and the
arms long enough to hook over your ears comfortably.

A lot of sunglasses will usually have sizing numbers on the inside of one of the arms. They look like: `53 □ 18  140`.
These refer to various measurements in millimeters (mm):

- Lens width (40mm to 60mm)
- Bridge width (14-24)
- Arm / Temple length (120mm to 150mm)

Medium-sized sunglasses are typically around `53 □ 18  140`.

## Protection

Sunglasses should shade your eyes from the sun, but also protect against UV radiation. Amount that sunglasses shade from
the sun is up to personal taste, but be careful with very shaded sunglasses when driving.

"UV400" is the recommended protection against UV radiation. This protects against UVA and UVB radiation:

| Property                        | UVA                         | UVB                       | UVC                           |
|---------------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Wavelength                      | 315nm to 400nm              | 280nm to 315nm            | 100nm to 280nm                |
| How bad?                        | Sunburn + some skin cancers | Bad sunburn + skin cancer | Severe sunburns + skin cancer |
| Absorbed by Earth's atmosphere? | Not really                  | Mostly (but not entirely) | Completely (Thanks, Earth!)   |

In Europe, sunglasses should have the [CE mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CE_marking) on them, which means they
conform with EU health, safety and environment standards - which means they protect against UVA and UVB radiation.

Sunglasses are covered by ISO standards (which have been adopted by the
EU): [ISO 12311](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/#iso:std:iso:12311:ed-2:v1:en)
and [ISO 12312](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/#iso:std:iso:12312:-3:ed-1:v1:en).

### Polarised

Polarisation is simply another way to filter light, specifically by filtering wavelengths that are horizontally
oriented.

This is particularly useful for reducing glare from water, which (apparently) can cause sunlight to be reflected
horizontally.

LCD screens typically emit polarised light, meaning polarised sunglasses can filter out that light and make it difficult
to view and use LCD screens. This can be useful for blocking out LCD advertisements, but otherwise is a slight
hindrance.

## Durability

Generally, glasses made of stronger materials like metal and decent wood or plastics are durable.

Cheap sunglasses made of flimsy plastic, will be less durable.

Thin arms and frames tend to be an indication of fragility. If you really want thin frames, it's best to keep your
sunglasses in a case when not wearing them.

## Sustainability

Wood, metal and glass are all materials that are either biodegradable or recyclable.

Plastics are obviously not acceptable here - if you are buying plastics, then at try to buy sustainably by purchasing
second-hand sunglasses.

Try to repair your sunglasses, instead of replacing them.

When it's time to dispose of your sunglasses, if they are in good condition, consider donating them to a second-hand
store, or selling them yourself. If they're broken and beyond repair, break them down into their parts and have them
recycled.

## Aesthetic

There is a lot of recommendations for what sunglasses look best on what faces. Generally speaking:

- If you have an angular face, then more rounded sunglasses are recommended.
- If you have a rounder face, more angular / squarer sunglasses are recommended.

This means your features are not accentuated, but softened, making them less jarring aesthetically - unless you want
that.

In my opinion, wayfarer-style sunglasses are quite universally "good".
