# Advice & Research Overview

> *Caveat emptor* (Latin)
>
> Let the buyer beware.

While consumer rights are strong in some countries, it's generally still important to have information and think things
through. The more informed you are, the better.

Here is a bunch of advice, research and reviews that I've done to hopefully help.

## Don't trust brand reputation

Brands having a reputation used to be a sign of trust and increased likelihood that you would get a good product.

In the modern world, a brand having a good reputation is just an opportunity for companies to start to cut costs and
reduce the quality of goods and services but keep the price the same (or higher). If a brand has a good reputation then
_verify_ it, by looking for the factors that show good
quality. [Enshittification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshittification) is real, and is happening more.

## Be sustainable

When thinking about purchasing something, remember to think and act sustainably.

Always opt for [sustainable materials](./sustainable-materials.md).

Be sceptical. Don't trust or prefer brands. Brands have repeatedly been shown to capitalise on trust and image, by
reducing product quality in order to increase
profits. [Enshittification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshittification) is real, wide-spread and sucks.

Everything is ultimately disposable, so whenever you're about to purchase something think "How am I going to dispose of
this without harming the environment?". If answering that question is difficult, unsustainable or bad for the
environment (e.g. going to landfill, will produce a bunch of microplastics, etc.), then try finding a better option for
what you're about to buy - again opting for things made
from [sustainable materials](./sustainable-materials.md) make answering this question MUCH easier.

Sustainable choices, in order of priority:

- **Don't buy it.** Reduce your consumption, which reduces need for production and waste. If you don't need it, why buy
  it?
- **Repair or alter existing**. If you have what you need, but it's broken, old or missing a feature: Try repairing it
  or altering it to fit your needs.
- **Buy second-hand (third-hand, etc.).** No need to buy new and require new materials to be used, if what you're
  looking for already exists and someone else wants to sell/trade it or give it away. Second-hand can be
  doubly-advantageous as a well-used item that is still in good condition can be a sign of a high quality item.
- **Make it yourself.** If you have the skills, and can obtain the materials - why not just make it yourself? This can
  save you money, be a fun hobby/side-project and will likely make you more attached to the final product.
- **Buy from small-scale, local makers.** This supports your local community, your local economy, and typically has a
  much smaller carbon footprint. You can often get a good deal, and more tailor-made products.
- **Buy high quality, sustainably-made, mass-produced.** High quality leads to a longer product life. Mass-produced just
  means not tailored. Sustainably-made means that manufacturer pays fair wages, has good working conditions and
  uses [sustainable materials](./sustainable-materials.md).
- **Stop here.** Don't buy low-quality or unsustainably made stuff!

This is basically "Reduce, reuse, recycle - in that order" but more explanatory.
