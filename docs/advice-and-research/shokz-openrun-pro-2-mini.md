# Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 Mini

I used to use [Google Pixel Buds](./2nd-gen-google-pixel-buds.md), but then I went through 3 pairs of those, because
they weren't very durable / I was clumsy in two ways:

1. Occasionally, I would drop one on the ground and after a few times the audio would just stop working in the ear bud.
2. After earwax had built up on the buds, I would clean them with a tooth pick and micro-fibre cloth and during the
   cleaning process, they would break.

So I started investigating for a new set of wireless earphones (not headphones), and landed on the _Shokz OpenRun Pro 2
Mini_. Here's how I got there and my review so far.

## My requirements

...for wireless earphones.

- Use for running and every-day listening.
- Fit securely on or in my ears.
- Connect with multiple devices, but mainly Android and a Garmin running watch (so basically: connect via Bluetooth)
- Have a decent battery life (1 day or more, ideally a few days at least).
- Charge via replaceable battery or via USB-C.
- Have some kind of noise pass-through, or able to turn off noise-cancelling, to allow for hearing traffic / things
  around me whilst using them.
- Have decent audio - not audiophile quality, or even excellent audio, just... acceptable - since I'll be running /
  commuting and want to hear what's around me anyway, so the audio quality is already massively affected by that.
- Have a microphone, that is decent sounding.
- Good / intuitive controls for volume and play/pausing audio.
- Be durable.
- Be repairable (be able to take apart, replace battery, replace parts etc.)

## Options

...at the time of buying:

- [Fairbuds](https://shop.fairphone.com/fairbuds) by _Fairphone_
	- Repairable (replaceable battery)
	- Saw reviews online and read
	  the [RTings review](https://www.rtings.com/headphones/reviews/fairphone/fairbuds-true-wireless) that showed decent
	  audio and controls, but had mixed microphone quality, poor comfort and a very low battery life (5 hours).
	- I already own a Fairphone, so I would expect good integration.
	- Bluetooth connectivity.
	- Seemed to sit quite far out the ear, which looked odd but also did not inspire confidence that they would stay in
	  place.
- A [OpenRun Pro 2 Mini](https://shokz.com/products/openrunpro2) by _Shokz_
	- Audio pass-through is part of the design (bone conduction rather than in-ear).
	- Decent audio quality (My friend had a OpenRun 1st generation, and it sounded fine, and the OpenRun Pro 2 had
	  actual speakers added for improved audio quality).
	- Hooked over ears seemed more secure than in-ears, especially for running.
	- Thinking the Mini because I wanted a closer fit to my head (I have a big head so the Mini might have been too
	  small, but trying on the normal size, I found it too big _for me_, so the Mini seems good).
	- Designed for running, so I would expect better water resistance / sweat
	  proofing. They are "sweatproof": [IP55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_code) rated.
	- Not that repairable (no easily replaceable battery, but given the only option WITH a replaceable battery (that I
	  could find) was the Fairbuds... then I can't really fulfil this requirement without the Fairbuds).
	- Good long battery life (9-12 hour), and charging via standard USB-C (not the Shokz proprietary connector as on
	  other models).
	- Intuitive controls (volume buttons and an action button for play/pause/skip-track/back-track)

So, I went for the _OpenRun Pro 2 Mini_ by _Shokz_!

## Findings

It's pretty good!

The listening experience was a little odd at first because I could clearly hear audio and my surroundings, but I got
used to it.

Wearing the device also felt odd (hooked over ears and gently pressing in just in front of my ears), but I got used to
that too, and now I sometimes forget that I'm wearing them. The Mini was a good decision though, it's a closer fit to my
head whilst not being too snug, but close enough that it doesn't catch on a collar or on things when I rest my head
against something.

Seems durable (not broken yet), though the volume switches sometimes don't work and just spam "Battery High" in my ears
over and over again.

I like the bone-conduction, though I do sometimes miss the "block out" that in-ear earphones and headphones provide
either naturally or with noise-cancelling... but in those cases I'm usually in a static location, so then I just plug in
some wired in-ear earphones (no battery, no bluetooth, just pure plug-in-cable-and-it-works goodness) or use headphones.

I look a little odd, in that the two black "speakers" make me look like I've got a sideburns.
