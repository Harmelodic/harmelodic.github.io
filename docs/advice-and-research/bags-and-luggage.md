# Bags and Luggage

Bags are useful to put things in when travelling.

## General criteria

- **Sustainable materials > Plastic or Synthetics.** All things are ultimately disposable, so buy &
  use [sustainable materials](sustainable-materials.md). Extra water-proofing can be achieved with applying
  a water-repelling finish (e.g. wax or non-PFAS coating).

- **Durable > Disposable.** Keep your bags lasting a long time! If you pick low-durable bags, you end up using weak,
  shitty bags and disposing of them regularly - which produces more waste and that's shit for the environment.

- **Closed > Open.** Closed bags (draw-strings or zips) keep your stuff dry when it rains and prevents petty theft. Open
  bags can be useful when needing to quickly pack and unpack, but bags with an open-bag style but have a draw-string or
  zip aren't too difficult to find and are still pretty easy to quickly pack and unpack.

- **Pack light.** Most of the time, you don't need all the stuff you think you need. Leaving it behind means you carry
  less and can use smaller, lighter bags.

## Functional bags

Don't just buy bags because they look cool. Buy bags because they provide a functional use. For me, there are specific
cases when I need a bag, and what sort of bags work in those cases. These are listed below, in order of _smallest_ to
_largest_ bags needed.

### Wallet / Safety pouch

Wallet for containing money and important documents (ID, passport, tickets/receipts).

I like having zip / secure pockets in my clothes for carrying these, to prevent pickpocketing. Some prefer a money
pouch - which is obviously useful if you don't have secure pockets.

### Commuting / Remote Work

When commuting to/from work or needing to carry my computer, a charger, some documents, stationary, a couple of small
items (e.g. including a reusable shopping bag).

This bag should:

- Fit the above general criteria (sustainable materials, durable, closed)
- Allow you to be hands-free - for better mobility.
- Be quickly accessible.

Use a _**satchel**_ with a laptop pouch/section inside or a _**small, light backpack**_ that is big enough to hold a
laptop/documents.

- A satchel is more easily accessible than a backpack, but a backpack usually allows for more mobility.
- Both can easily be found in leather or canvas for durability.

### Day pack when travelling

When travelling, I often have much larger bags/suitcases to carry most of my belongings. However, once settled in a
hotel, hostel or campsite, it's useful to have a bag when out and about during the day, without needing to lug about my
larger bags, to hold things like water, a water-repellent jacket, snacks, first-aid supplies, etc.

This bag should be:

- Fit the above general criteria (sustainable materials, durable, closed)
- Allow you to be hands-free - for better mobility.
- Be reasonably easy to access - so that it's not a hassle to grab your things from inside whilst out and about.
- Be able to be compacted - so that it can be fit inside a larger bag when not in use whilst travelling.

For this, use either a _**very light, small backpack**_ that can be compacted down when not containing anything, or a
_**foldable bag**_ that unfolds to being 20 to 25 litre backpack (this is big enough to hold everything, but small
enough to retain being small when folded).

- Light backpacks are great for mobility and reasonably easy to access.
- A very light, small backpack that can be compacted will likely have more structure and be more comfortable than a
  foldable bag, but a foldable bag will likely compact better.
- Opt for durable but easily foldable fabrics like thick linen and cotton (e.g. denim). Avoid overly stiff fabrics like
  leather or canvas as they won't compact well.
- Zips or drawstrings are both good for keeping them closed.
- Try to get one with a side pocket for holding a water bottle.

### Specialised bags

Packing cubes are great for organising your stuff in larger bags, rather than stuffing it all in and hoping it fits.
Having a spare one to be used for dirty laundry is useful.

When needing to transport fancy clothes like suits or dresses, a dedicated "suit bag" is preferable as it will fold in
the correct way for travel and minimising creases, have openings available for using hangers, and often have small
pockets for accessories.

Similarly, a small fabric bag for carrying smart shoes is useful, rather than scuffing or damaging your smart shoes by
storing them with other items.

### 1-3 day trips

When your travelling and going away for the weekend or maybe your going for a hike or day trip and need to carry a bunch
of stuff - it's useful to have a _**backpack**_.

This backpack should:

- Fit the above general criteria (sustainable materials, durable, closed)
- 25 to 30 litre backpack. 25 litres is ideal, as often 25 litres is the maximum volume that you can carry for bags that
  would fit underneath an airplane seat.
- Adjustable straps are vital to ensure a good fit. A hip belt can be useful for shifting weight onto your hips rather
  than it being on your shoulders, for added comfort if you're going for a hike.
- External pockets on the outside, ideally elasticated, are useful for storing water bottles and other quick-to-hand
  items. **Don't** get a bag that has outside pockets that sit flush with the rest of the bag or are "internal" pockets
  accessible from the outside, as when the bag is full, the pockets won't be able to store anything! It may look slick /
  cool / minimalist, but those pockets become functionally useless.
- Have daisy chains, PALS (pouch attachment ladder system) and/or gear loops on the outside of your bag are also useful
  for attaching extra gear or accessories. I recommend attaching things to these using small carabiners, loops of string
  or backpack compression straps.
- Inside, it's best to have a simple large open space for, rather than a bag that has been broken up into sections "for
  helping organisation". This is because a single large open space is much more versatile, and you can organise your
  stuff using packing cubes (as mentioned in [specialised bags](#specialised-bags)). However, a slim section that runs
  down the back of the bag is useful for carrying a laptop or water/hydration bladder.
- Ideally, the inside of the back should be a bright colour, like orange or yellow, which helps to spot things when your
  searching your bag for something.

### Multi-day hikes and backpacking

Depending on how big you are, how much you can carry and how much you need to carry, a big 45 to 70 litre backpack is
what shines best here.

This backpack should:

- Fit the above general criteria (sustainable materials, durable, closed)
- Be 45 to 70 litres in space. You can also get ones that have expandable volume (e.g. labelled "45L + 15L").
- Have adjustable straps in order to achieve good comfort and ergonomics when wearing.
- Have a sturdy frame gives support and structure to the backpack so that it doesn't jostle around resulting in extra
  strain on your shoulder, back and core muscles.
- Have a hip belt, in order to distribute the weight onto your hips and shoulders. Hip belts often come with pockets
  which are useful for carrying quick-access items, like tissues and snacks.
- External pockets on the outside, ideally elasticated, are useful for storing water bottles and other quick-to-hand
  items. **Don't** get a bag that has outside pockets that sit flush with the rest of the bag or are "internal" pockets
  accessible from the outside, as when the bag is full, the pockets won't be able to store anything! It may look slick /
  cool / minimalist, but those pockets become functionally useless.
- Have daisy chains, PALS (pouch attachment ladder system) and/or gear loops on the outside of your bag are also useful
  for attaching extra gear or accessories. I recommend attaching things to these using small carabiners, loops of string
  or backpack compression straps.
- Have compression straps on the outside, for tightening the backpacking when full and ensuring that your stuff (and
  therefore the backpack) doesn't jostle around a lot which can be uncomfortable and strain your shoulder & back
  and core muscles.
- Have a hardy flat bottom, so that when you put the backpack down on the ground, it doesn't immediately fall over, and
  makes it much easier to pack and unpack.
- Have a top lid that can be loosened and tightened, so that you can put jackets or rolled-up sleeping mats/pads
  underneath and strap them down.
- Be wax coated for waterproofing, or come with a backpack cover for protecting the pack from the rain. Don't bother
  getting a "waterproof" backpack as they will usually lose their waterproofing over time, and the waterproofing that is
  offered usually comes from being made from synthetic, non-sustainable materials, or have a PFAS or other coating that
  is not good for the environment.
- Have multiple points of entry (usually: Top, front and bottom) for access to your stuff stored in different parts of
  your bag.
- Inside, it's best to have a simple large open space for, rather than a bag that has been broken up into sections "for
  helping organisation". This is because a single large open space is much more versatile, and you can organise your
  stuff using packing cubes (as mentioned in [specialised bags](#specialised-bags)). However, a slim section that runs
  down the back of the bag is useful for carrying a laptop or water/hydration bladder.

### Comfortable travelling

When travelling in comfort, a large suitcase or large wheeled duffel bag is best, as you won't be carrying it a lot.

A travelling suitcase is often easier to manoeuvre but is usually made of synthetic materials. A duffel bag is usually
more difficult to manoeuvre but can be made of canvas.

A suitcase / duffel bag should:

- Fit the above general criteria (sustainable materials, durable, closed).
- Be a bright colour or unique style or be decorated with bright straps for easy identification.
- Be as big as you need it, but not oversized to the point where maneuverability is hindered.
- Have _replaceable wheels_, so that when they inevitably break or stick, you can repair or replace them - rather than
  buying a whole new bag when one of the wheels break.
- If opting for a suitcase, get one with 4 small swivelable wheels. Four wheels means you can either drag or roll the
  suitcase. Small wheels are less likely to break off or receive damage during transport. Swivelable wheels also aid
  manoeuvrability.
- If opting for a duffel bag, get one with 2 static wheels. A duffel bag will have to be dragged, so having more or
  swivelable wheels will either be useless or create more instability.
- Have a sturdy, telescopic, multi-height adjustable handle. Sturdy because it will likely be under some tension/strain
  when pulling the suitcase. Telescopic to make storage and usage easier. Multi-height adjustable to allow you to raise
  or lower the handle to a level that is most ergonomic for you. Suitcases often come with 2 or 3 points where the
  handle can lock in.
- Has a TSA-approved lock on it for locking the zippers, but allowing the TSA to open it if required for security
  purposes (TSA-approved locks can be opened with a universal key that the TSA have). This can deter theft, whilst
  ensuring that the TSA doesn't break your lock if they need to access your luggage for security screening.
- Contain an internal compression strap for securing internal items.
- Contain an internal mesh pocket on the "lid" of the suitcase for storing slim items slightly separately, like
  toiletries or a tracking tag.
- Inside, it's best to have a simple large open space for, rather than a bag that has been broken up into sections "for
  helping organisation". This is because a single large open space is much more versatile, and you can organise your
  stuff using packing cubes (as mentioned in [specialised bags](#specialised-bags)). However, a slim section that runs
  down the back of the bag is useful for carrying a laptop or water/hydration bladder.
- Have an outside pocket for holding items or paperwork that you might need to quickly access.
- Be somewhat expandable (usually being able to unzip a part to allow for the suitcase have an increased depth) for
  longer trips where extra storage is required.
- Be large enough to contain folded suit/dress bags, so that you don't need to carry your suit bag separately.
