# Sustainable Hardware

Modern consumerism & capitalism has promoted the following behaviour, when it comes to a lot of consumer technology /
hardware:

> Buy thing, use thing, {insert reason for throwing thing away}, toss thing. Repeat.

Where the reasons could be: it broke, or you dislike it, or predatory marketing practices convince you that you dislike
it or that you need a new one, and so on.

Instead, it's plain better and more sustainable to instead be able to upgrade, modify, and repair the hardware. That's
sustainable hardware. That's true _ownership_ of your stuff.

To be able to upgrade, modify and repair your stuff, we need the following:

- Modular design, to allow for upgrades and modifications to be easier.
- Be designed to be easy to take apart (e.g. standard screws, non-soldered wire connections, clips and screw-in
  components rather than glue) to allow for upgrades, modifications and repairs to be easier.
- Have schematics available for easy troubleshooting, and allowing it to be easy to design modifications or upgrades of
  your own.
- Have guides available for how to do upgrades, modifications and repairs.
- Someone (usually the manufacturer) to provide replacement parts - mainly for repairs.
- Ideally, publish designs and other technical information as being open source, so that people are free from legal
  issues when trying to upgrade, modify or repair their stuff, or help others do the same.

Check out [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/) for guides and pointers on how to repair your stuff,
and [The Repair Association](https://www.repair.org) for how to help shape better legislation that promotes the "Right
to Repair".

## Examples

- [Framework](https://frame.work) laptop & desktop computers.
- [Fairphone](https://www.fairphone.com) phones, headphones, and earbuds.
- [Open Funk](https://www.openfunk.co)'s re:Mix blender. 
- Build your own computer.
