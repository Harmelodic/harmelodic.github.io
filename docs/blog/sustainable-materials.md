# Sustainable Materials

## Overview

Everything is ultimately disposable, so it's important for us to use sustainable materials. This means using mostly
*organic* materials, or minerals that can be repaired or recycled easily. Some of these materials include:

- Cotton
- Linen
- Hemp
- Denim (cotton)
- Canvas (cotton, linen, hemp)
- Wool
- Silk
- Bamboo
- Wicker (willow, rattan, reeds, bamboo)
- Leather, or vegan (apple) leather
- Cork
- Natural rubber
- Metals
- Wood
- Glass
- Natural resin
- Paper

⚠️ Plastic fabrics produce microplastics (not good) through wear and washing. They also tend to not feel soft or nice to
the touch, and can become smelly easily as a lot of them are not antibacterial. Why do companies use them? It's cheap.
Don't encourage this behaviour buying or using plastic / synthetic fabrics.

I'm not going to go too much into material engineering, but here's some especially useful things to know:

## Wool and Merino wool is excellent

- Wool aids [thermoregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoregulation) by the wool fibres forming pockets for
  air which is insulating and keeps your warm, and helps wicks away sweat effectively which prevents overheating.
- Wool [is antibacterial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8950207/) (prevents growth of bacteria) leading you
  to not smell for longer, as bacterial growth is what makes you smell.
- It's an organic material and will degrade well when eventually disposed off.
- Wool fibres from the outer layer of the wool-producing animal are itchier and typically cheaper. This is good for
  layers of clothing which won't be in contact with your skin.
- Wool fibres from the inner layer of the wool-producing animal are softer and typically more expensive. This is good
  for base layers and clothing that touches your skin.
- Merino wool is fine (thin), soft, lightweight and slightly stretchy which hugely improves comfort.

## Other useful knowledge

Linen is super light and breathable and thus great for clothing designed for hot climates.

Cork is light, hard but soft enough to be malleable, and often floats.

Leather stretches over time, so if you're getting shoes made from leather start _slightly_ tight to let it stretch to
achieve very comfortable fit.

Wood, Metals, Wicker and Glass are good alternatives to hard plastics.

Soft but hardy fabrics like Silk, Denim or Canvas are good alternatives to soft plastics. For items designed to be
"disposable" that currently use soft plastics, could instead be paper.

Natural rubber and cork are good alternatives to firm plastics.

Biodegradable bioplastics _could_ be used for items where other more sustainable materials are a poor fit, however some
processes for producing bioplastics actually have a higher carbon footprint than plastics made from fossil-fuels.
